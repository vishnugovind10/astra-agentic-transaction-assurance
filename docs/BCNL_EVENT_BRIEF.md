## Published panels

### Panel 1 — How will AI Agents pay each other?
Speakers listed on the event page include representatives from Visa, GoDutch, Arios and Adyen.

Astra relevance:
- agent identity versus legal/commercial identity;
- buyer/seller relationship;
- payee wallet binding;
- machine payment authority;
- commercial obligation and settlement evidence.

Questions:
1. What is the hardest unsolved trust problem after an agent has received payment authority?
2. How do payment systems bind the intended business counterparty to a receiving wallet?
3. Who verifies that a payment corresponds to the agreed service or invoice?
4. What needs to be independently auditable when one agent pays another?

### Panel 2 — Who controls the infrastructure behind AI?
The event page lists speakers associated with Nosana, TradeZero and Picnic.

Astra relevance:
- agents purchasing GPU/compute, data and inference;
- usage metering and cost evidence;
- provider identity and wallet association;
- repeated/failed jobs and duplicate charges;
- settlement tied to job/quote references.

Questions:
1. How are compute jobs quoted, metered and evidenced today?
2. Can a buyer agent verify the provider and receiving wallet before payment?
3. What evidence is returned to distinguish successful execution from a paid but failed job?
4. How are retries and duplicate job claims accounted for?

### Panel 3 — Are we ready to let AI run our organisation?
The event page lists speakers from Prosus, Quantfyi and AI & Partners.

Astra relevance:
- principal authority and delegation;
- cumulative economic exposure;
- accountable autonomous procurement;
- evidence and reconciliation requirements;
- boundaries between technical autonomy and legal/commercial authority.

Questions:
1. What controls must remain under explicit organisational authority?
2. What prevents an agent's locally valid actions from creating an economically unacceptable aggregate outcome?
3. What evidence would treasury, risk or audit require before allowing autonomous payments?

## Product hypotheses to validate

1. Payment authorisation alone is insufficient for enterprise agent-to-agent commerce.
2. Counterparty/payee binding is a distinct problem from agent identity.
3. Cumulative exposure and duplicate commercial claims matter when multiple agents procure services.
4. Provider receipts and on-chain settlement need correlation and independent reconciliation.
5. Payment platforms or marketplaces may prefer an external assurance module rather than build every control themselves.

## Event discipline

Do not pitch the full architecture indiscriminately. Ask discovery questions, establish current workflow and evidence requirements, identify the accountable buyer, and request a follow-up technical conversation only where a specific problem is confirmed.
